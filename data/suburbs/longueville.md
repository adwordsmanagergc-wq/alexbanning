Longueville occupies a peninsula on the Lane Cove River. It is a small suburb with no through traffic, reached by Longueville Road from Lane Cove village, and that geography defines everything about it. The streets are quiet, the trees are established, and a large share of homes have either water frontage or a view of the river.

This is one of the more private addresses on the lower north shore. Homes sit well back from the road behind hedges and gates. Many waterfront properties run down to the river with their own landing or jetty, and the bays around the peninsula are used for kayaking, rowing and small boats. Lane Cove village, with its shops, cafes and services, is a short drive or a longer walk away.

The housing stock is varied and often significant. There are federation and interwar houses on large parcels, grand family homes from later decades, and a number of architect designed contemporary residences that take full advantage of the slope and the outlook. Some properties have been rebuilt, others have been held and cared for by the same family for a generation or more.

Selling here is rarely about volume. The buyer pool is smaller and more considered: established families, executives relocating within Sydney, returning expatriates and downsizers from larger estates who still want water and space. Many prefer to inspect privately before a home is widely advertised. **Discretion matters**, both to sellers and to the buyers they attract.

That makes preparation important. Quality photography at the right time of day, aerial imagery that shows the frontage and the river, and clear information on waterfront structures and approvals help buyers commit with confidence. Campaigns in Longueville often combine quiet early conversations with a measured public launch, so that the right buyers are reached without the home being overexposed.

## FAQ

### Is auction or private treaty better in Longueville?
It depends on the home and the depth of interest. Auction can work well for waterfront and prestige homes where several qualified buyers are likely, because it creates a firm deadline. Private treaty or an expressions of interest process can suit unique properties where buyers need more time for due diligence on land, structures and approvals. Many Longueville sellers decide after gauging early interest.

### Should I sell off market in Longueville?
An off market approach can suit sellers who value privacy, or homes with an obvious buyer already in view. The trade off is exposure. Without a public campaign, you may not test the full market, and a buyer who would have paid more may never see the home. A good compromise is a short private phase with a clear plan to launch publicly if needed.

### What should I prepare for a waterfront sale?
Buyers will ask about the frontage. Gather any approvals and licences for jetties, pontoons, boatsheds and seawalls, along with maintenance records and survey information. Buyers and their advisers will review these closely, and having them ready shortens the due diligence period and reduces the risk of late negotiations or delays.

### When is the best time to sell in Longueville?
Spring and early autumn are traditionally busy, and gardens and river light often show well then. But a prestige home with a small buyer pool does not always benefit from a crowded season. Timing around your own plans, and around the buyers already in the market, often matters more than the calendar.
