Lane Cove West lies between Lane Cove village and the Lane Cove River, a pocket of family streets threaded with bushland reserves and creeks that run down to the water. It has a quieter, more residential feel than the village itself, while remaining close enough to walk or take a short bus ride for shops, cafes and services.

Burns Bay Road runs through the suburb and carries buses towards the city and the village, as well as local traffic. Off that corridor, streets curve along ridges and down into gullies, and many homes look out over treetops rather than neighbours. Foreshore reserves and walking tracks give residents easy access to the river, which is used for kayaking and small boats. Lane Cove West Public School is a focal point for young families.

The housing stock is predominantly detached. Post-war and mid-century houses on sloping blocks are common, many extended over the years, and there is a steady stream of contemporary rebuilds that take advantage of bush and river outlooks. Townhouses and apartments add options for downsizers and first home buyers who want to stay close to the Lane Cove community.

Buyers are mostly families. Many are upsizing from apartments or smaller homes nearby, often from Lane Cove, St Leonards or Chatswood, and they prioritise bedrooms, outdoor space, schools and a quiet street. Owner builders look for sites with a good aspect and outlook. Downsizers who already live in the area tend to stay within it, seeking lower maintenance homes.

**For sellers, the story is space and setting.** Photography that shows the garden, the outlook and the sense of privacy speaks directly to family buyers. For homes on steep blocks, clear floor plans and honest information about access and levels help buyers understand the property. A well organised campaign, priced realistically, suits a market where buyers compare closely and move decisively for the right home.

## FAQ

### What do buyers look for in Lane Cove West?
Families look for space, a practical layout, outdoor areas children can use and proximity to schools and parks. A quiet street and a bush or river outlook add appeal. Buyers also consider access, parking and the effort needed to maintain a sloping garden, so clear information on these points is helpful.

### Is auction or private treaty better in Lane Cove West?
Auction suits well presented family homes where competition is likely, because it gives buyers a clear deadline. Private treaty can suit homes needing major work, properties with steep or unusual sites, and apartments or townhouses. Your agent should recommend a method based on early interest and the nature of the property.

### When is the best time to sell in Lane Cove West?
Spring is traditionally busy and bushland gardens often show well. Family buyers may also time moves around the school year, which can create demand at other points. Consider your own plans, the level of competing stock and how quickly your home can be prepared, rather than relying on season alone.

### How do I present a home on a sloping block?
Show buyers how the home works. A clear floor plan, photographs of each level and good images of the garden and outlook help. Tidy paths and steps, improve lighting and make sure outdoor areas look usable. Be upfront about access, as buyers will notice it at inspection anyway, and clarity builds trust.
