Chatswood is the commercial heart of the lower north shore. Its rail and Metro interchange connects it to the city, North Sydney, Macquarie Park and beyond, and its retail core, anchored by Westfield and Chatswood Chase, draws shoppers from across the region. The Concourse, with its theatre, concert hall and library, gives the centre a cultural focus, and Victoria Avenue's pedestrian mall is busy through the day and into the evening.

Yet Chatswood is two places at once. Around the station, apartment towers and mixed use buildings define the skyline. Step a few streets east of the centre and the character changes completely. Tree lined streets hold federation and interwar houses, many within heritage conservation areas, with deep front gardens and a calm residential feel.

That contrast creates two distinct markets. The apartment market is broad, drawing professionals, couples, downsizers, investors and buyers who value walking distance to transport, shops and restaurants. The house market is smaller and competitive, driven largely by families who want character homes with access to well regarded schools, including Chatswood Public School and Chatswood High School, and the convenience of the centre nearby.

Chatswood's buyer pool is diverse and well informed. Many buyers research carefully, compare buildings and streets in detail, and move quickly when a property meets their brief. Local buyers upgrading within the suburb are common, as are buyers relocating from elsewhere in Sydney and overseas.

**For sellers, clarity is everything.** Apartment campaigns should lead with light, layout, outlook, parking and the quality of the building, with strata records ready from the first inspection. Heritage house campaigns should show the street, the garden and the period detail, with accurate information on conservation controls. In both markets, realistic pricing and responsive communication with buyers make a measurable difference to the result.

## FAQ

### What do buyers look for in Chatswood apartments?
Light, a sensible layout, secure parking and storage are the fundamentals. Buyers weigh the building closely: its age, maintenance, strata levies and any history of defects. Walking distance to the station and Metro is a major factor. Views and a quiet aspect add appeal. Have strata reports and building information ready to support a confident offer.

### Is auction or private treaty better in Chatswood?
Houses, especially heritage homes in conservation areas, often suit auction because several families may compete for the same property. Apartments are more commonly sold by private treaty, where buyers compare options across buildings. The best method depends on the property type, the level of demand and how much certainty you want over timing.

### How do heritage conservation areas affect selling a Chatswood house?
Conservation controls limit demolition and shape what can be changed at the front of a house. Many buyers seek these streets precisely because the character is protected. Provide clear information about the controls and any past approvals, so buyers can plan with confidence and the sale is not delayed by late questions.

### Should I sell my Chatswood apartment with a tenant in place?
It can work, particularly for investors who want immediate income. But a tenanted apartment is harder to present and inspect, and owner occupiers may prefer vacant possession. If possible, consider timing the sale around the end of a lease, and make sure tenants are informed and cooperative throughout the campaign.
