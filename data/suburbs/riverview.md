Riverview is a small residential suburb on the Lane Cove River, between Lane Cove and Hunters Hill across the water. It is best known as the home of Saint Ignatius' College, whose grounds and historic buildings occupy a large part of the suburb and give it a distinctive, established character.

The rest of Riverview is quiet and residential. Streets wind down towards the river and its bays, including Tambourine Bay, with bushland reserves and foreshore walks between the houses. There is little through traffic, and the neighbourhood has a settled, family feel. Lane Cove village is close for shopping and cafes, and buses connect to the city.

The housing stock is mostly detached. Post-war and mid-century houses remain common, often on generous blocks with gardens that follow the slope. Many have been extended or rebuilt, and contemporary family homes now sit alongside original brick houses. On the water, a smaller number of residences take in river outlooks and have direct foreshore access.

Buyers are predominantly families. Proximity to schools, including Saint Ignatius', is a significant draw, and many buyers are already connected to the area through a child, a relative or a previous home nearby. Upsizers from apartments in Lane Cove and St Leonards are active, as are owner builders looking for a good site. Downsizers tend to stay close, often moving to Lane Cove or a smaller home within Riverview.

For sellers, this means the campaign should speak to family life. Bedrooms, living areas, outdoor space and a practical layout matter more than grand finishes. **Clear floor plans and honest condition information** help buyers who are weighing renovation against rebuilding. Photography that shows the garden, the street and any glimpse of the river gives buyers a real sense of the setting.

## FAQ

### Is auction or private treaty better in Riverview?
Auction suits well presented family homes where competition among buyers is likely, particularly when school timing creates urgency. Private treaty can suit homes needing major work, where builders and renovators may need time to assess costs. Your agent should recommend a method based on early interest and the property itself.

### How do school catchments affect selling in Riverview?
Many buyers choose Riverview for schools, both private and public. For public schools, check current catchment boundaries with the Department of Education, as they can change. Present this information accurately in your campaign. Buyers will verify it, and precise information builds trust while overstatement undermines it.

### Should I renovate or sell as is in Riverview?
If the home is structurally sound but dated, a light refresh is usually more effective than a major renovation. Buyers here often plan to extend or rebuild and would rather pay for their own design. Repairs, paint, garden work and a clean presentation help most. Seek advice before committing to larger works.

### When is the best time to sell in Riverview?
Family buyers often aim to move around school terms, so timing a campaign to allow settlement before a new year or term can be useful. Spring is traditionally busy, but quality homes can sell well at other times too. Your personal timing and the level of competing stock matter as much as the season.
